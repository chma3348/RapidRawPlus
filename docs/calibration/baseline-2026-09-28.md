# Calibration baseline, 28 September 2026

Every export in Chris's `Davinci Test` package (16 sources, 200 exports)
measured against v3 as the app renders it, at full resolution, before any
slider is fitted. Numbers are 8-bit levels: the mean absolute per-channel
difference and the signed mean per channel (ours minus Resolve). The
machine-readable form is `baseline-2026-09-28.json`; regenerate with
`color_v3_reference render … ` and `sweep` (see
`docs/color-v3-calibration-intake.md`).

What this says:

- **Shadows** is not a scale away. Resolve's ±50 is already beyond our
  ±100, and at our limit the residual is still 26–48 levels: Resolve's
  Photo-page Shadows reaches much further up the tonal range and much
  harder than v2's. New formula, fitted first.
- **Highlights** is stronger in Resolve too, most on the positive side
  (Resolve +50 ≈ our +100), and the residual at the best value (7–27) says
  the shape differs as well. Second.
- **Saturation** is close: Resolve 100 ≈ our 68, −100 ≈ our −89, residual
  4–6 levels. A mild, slightly asymmetric scale, probably a different
  saturation space. Third, quick.

## Held out

**Display P3 iPhone photos** (three): neutral differs by 2.4 levels with a
−3.2 red bias, because Resolve read them as sRGB and the app honours the
profile. Not fitted until they are re-tagged Display P3 in Resolve and the
P3 lattice capture is installed.

**RAW** (seven ARW): neutral differs by 21 levels mean. Resolve's Camera RAW
development relative to ours, in stops by the reference's own brightness

Three files sit a uniform 0.3 stop brighter (an exposure convention);
DSC03458 and DSC07955 are brighter by more in the shadows than the
highlights (a rolloff or an auto adjustment); the ILCE-9 pair differ from
the others. This is a decode matter, settled from Chris's Camera RAW panel
values, and kept out of the slider fit so the fit does not absorb it.
